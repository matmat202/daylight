Chapter 1 · Two easy answers

Not summer time, and not China

Summer time moves the clock an hour ahead of the sun for part of the year. Take it away, every clock held at its winter setting all year, and the sun still peaks after noon for ; the middle moves only to . Summer time is of it.

China keeps one clock across five thousand kilometres, so it might be China. Leave every Chinese town out and the share is : the same, give or take a point.

What is left is the zones themselves. Even with no summer time, of people have a clock half an hour or more ahead of their sun, and one half an hour behind it. When a clock is set wrong, it is set early.

Epilogue · Reading your own town

Lunch at noon is lunch before the sun

On Daylight, “sun highest” is this number for one town on one day. It wanders through the year by up to a quarter of an hour either way (the sun runs fast in autumn and slow in late winter), which is why this story takes the year’s mean.

Data: the site’s own GeoNames cities15000 towns (CC BY 4.0), of them, each weighted by its GeoNames population, and each town’s zone from the IANA tz database. The towns are not everyone: villages and towns under 15,000 people are left out. GeoNames puts Ürümqi and Kashgar on Xinjiang’s own clock, two hours behind Beijing’s, which many there keep; on Beijing time, the official one, their sun would be two hours later still. The sun’s position is the site’s own (NOAA’s equations). “No summer time” holds each zone at its smallest offset of the year. The program is in the repository.
